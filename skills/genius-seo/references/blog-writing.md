# World-Class Blog Writing: The Research-Backed Playbook

How to write posts people actually read, stay on and act on. Sources read 2026-11-05. Re-check before quoting.

## What the research says
**How people read** ([NN/g, "How Users Read on the Web"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/)):
- **79%** of test users always **scanned** a new page; only **16%** read word by word.
- Measured usability rose **58%** with **concise** text (about half the words), **47%** with **scannable** text, **27%** with **objective** (no-hype) language, and **124%** with all three combined.
- "Marketese" (promotional language) adds cognitive burden and readers dislike it.
- Scannable text means highlighted keywords, **meaningful (not clever) subheadings**, bulleted lists, **one idea per paragraph**, and the inverted pyramid.

**Inverted pyramid** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/inverted-pyramid/)): put the most important information first, so readers can "stop reading at any point… and still come away with the main point." "The first sentence of every paragraph should be the most important. The first words in each sentence should be information-carrying." A summary or key-points list at the top helps.

**People scan in patterns like the F-shape** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/)): they read the top, then skim down the left edge. Front-load headings, paragraphs and bullets.

**Plain language** ([Federal Plain Language Guidelines](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language)): write for your specific audience; use common, everyday words (necessary technical terms explained); address the reader as "you"; use the **active voice**; keep sentences, paragraphs and sections short.

**Google** ([people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)): no preferred word count ("No, we don't"). Value means original, beyond-the-obvious content, a descriptive non-clickbait title, clear sourcing, a visible author with real expertise, and a reader who leaves satisfied without searching again.

**What correlates with results** ([Orbit Media 2026 blogger survey](https://www.orbitmedia.com/blog/blogging-statistics/), 1,000+ marketers; correlation, not proof):
- Average post: **1,312 words**, **3 h 20 min** to write. Only **14%** report "strong results".
- Past surveys: **6+ hours on 2,000-word posts roughly doubled the odds** of strong results. This year's top group (31% strong results) **spent 6+ hours and used 4+ strategies per post**. "Faster isn't better."
- **2–3 visuals per post** is the most likely to report strong results.
- **Original research** improves the likelihood of performance by **50%**.
- **Human editors:** the human-editor group beats the benchmark by **nearly 2×**. **Using AI "correlates with nothing"** and doesn't improve performance.
- **Influencer or expert collaboration** is the single best predictor of success.
- **Harder promotion channels outperform easy ones.** Marketers who check analytics report more success, and **traffic is the weakest metric to track**.

**What "bounce" means now** ([GA4](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/12195621)): a session is **engaged** if it lasts **over 10 seconds**, **has a key event**, or has **2+ page views**. Bounce rate = the share of sessions that weren't engaged. So, to cut bounce: give a reason to stay past 10 seconds, a next page to visit, and a clear action (key event).

## The anatomy of a world-class post
1. **Title:** descriptive, specific and honest. The reader's problem or question plus the promise. Key words first. No clickbait (Google asks whether the title avoids "exaggerating or being shocking").
2. **Subtitle/dek:** one sentence on who it's for and what they'll get. This often becomes the meta description.
3. **Opening (first screen):** answer the question in the **first 2–3 sentences** (inverted pyramid). Name the reader's situation ("If you've applied to 100 jobs and…"). No throat-clearing.
4. **Key takeaways:** 3–6 bullets. A reader who stops here still wins.
5. **Body sections:** a **meaningful H2 for each sub-question**, written as the question or the answer. Each section is self-contained: answer first, then the explanation, example and steps.
6. **Proof and experience:** first-hand examples, scripts, numbers you can source, a mini case study (with permission), quotes from experts. **This is what makes it non-commodity.**
7. **Visuals:** **2–3 meaningful visuals**: a diagram, checklist graphic, annotated screenshot or a sourced chart. Each with descriptive alt text; never put key information *only* in an image.
8. **Next steps:** a simple plan or checklist the reader can act on today.
9. **Internal links:** contextual links to the next logical post and the matching service, written as descriptive anchors.
10. **One CTA:** the main conversion, stated as an outcome, with the friction removed (`conversion.md`).
11. **FAQ:** 4–6 real questions (People Also Ask, forums), answered in 1–3 sentences each.
12. **Trust footer:** author byline with background, "sources checked on [date]", and a disclaimer where needed (legal, medical, financial).

## Sentence-level rules
- **One idea per paragraph**, 1–4 sentences. **One idea per sentence**; most under ~20 words.
- **Active voice:** "Your employer files the petition", not "The petition is filed".
- **"You" and "we":** talk to one reader.
- **Common words:** use, help, start, not utilize, facilitate, commence. Explain jargon the first time.
- **Specific over vague:** "15–25 applications a week", not "lots of applications". Cite the number's source.
- **Objective, not hype:** no "revolutionary", "game-changing", "ultimate"; no exclamation marks.
- **Front-load:** the first 2–3 words of every heading, paragraph and bullet carry the meaning.
- **Bold sparingly:** the 1–2 phrases per section a scanner must see.
- **Lists** for steps, options and criteria. Not every line.

## Retention: keep readers on the page (and beat "bounce")
| Reader behavior | Fix |
|---|---|
| Leaves in the first seconds | The answer and the reader's situation in the first screen; a clear title-to-content match |
| Can't find their part | Descriptive H2s plus a table of contents ("Jump to section") |
| Gets lost in text | Short paragraphs, lists, white space, 2–3 visuals |
| Doubts the advice | Sources, dates, a named author with experience, real examples |
| Finishes and leaves (1 page view) | Contextual links to the next logical post; a "read next" section |
| Wants help but no action is clear | One visible CTA plus one soft offer (`conversion.md`) |
| Slow or jumpy page | Core Web Vitals "good": LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1 (`google-playbook.md`) |

## The process (from brief to refresh)
1. **Vet the keyword** (5 checks + AI filter) and check for cannibalization.
2. **Study the top 3 results:** what they all cover, what they miss, the format that wins.
3. **Find your original angle:** experience, data, scripts or a case study the others lack.
4. **Outline:** one H2 per sub-question, ordered by importance (inverted pyramid).
5. **Draft fast, then cut** about 30–50% (NN/g: concise text = +58% usability).
6. **Fact-check every claim** against primary sources, and date-stamp them.
7. **Human edit:** structure, clarity, tone, accuracy. Ideally a second person (Orbit: human editors ≈2× results).
8. **Add 2–3 visuals,** alt text, internal links and the CTA ladder.
9. **Pre-publish test** (below).
10. **Publish and distribute the same week:** LinkedIn, communities, newsletter, expert collaborators.
11. **Measure** engaged sessions, scroll and key events by post, and leads by post, not just traffic.
12. **Refresh** substantively when facts change, or after ~6 months without ranking. Never fake the date.

## Pre-publish test (score /100; publish at 85+)
| Area | Points | Pass if… |
|---|---|---|
| **Intent and value** | 20 | It matches what ranks (type, format, angle); it adds something original beyond the top 3 |
| **Opening** | 10 | The answer is in the first 2–3 sentences; the reader's situation is named; key takeaways are present |
| **Scannability** | 15 | Meaningful H2s, one idea per paragraph, lists where useful, front-loaded text, a TOC |
| **Plain language** | 10 | Active voice, common words, short sentences, no hype or marketese |
| **Accuracy and trust** | 15 | Every claim sourced and dated; author byline; no easily verified errors; disclaimers where needed |
| **Experience and proof** | 10 | First-hand examples, scripts, data or a case study |
| **Visuals and media** | 5 | 2–3 meaningful visuals with alt text; no key info only in images |
| **Retention** | 5 | Contextual internal links; a "read next"; a fast, stable page |
| **Conversion** | 10 | One service link in the body, one soft offer, one outcome-focused CTA, tracking fires |

**Automatic fail (don't publish):** an unsourced statistic, a factual error, a fake author, a page that duplicates an existing post's intent, or content made mainly to rank.

## Using AI responsibly
AI can help research, outline and draft, but it **correlates with nothing on its own** (Orbit), and Google requires a human fact-check of AI-assisted content, including titles, descriptions and alt text. Always add human experience, verify every fact, and edit for voice. Disclose substantial automation where readers would expect it.
